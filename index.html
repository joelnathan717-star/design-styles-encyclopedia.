1919 – 1933 WEIMAR • DESSAU
ARCHETYPE: WASSILY B3

Bauhaus & Functional Modernism

Radical synthesis of fine art and craft championing the elimination of ornamentation. The form emerges strictly from structural purpose, creating timeless geometric utility.

Materials: Tubular Steel, Bent Plywood, Cast Glass
Form: Pure Geometry, Asymmetric Balance
SWATCH
1955 – Present FRANKFURT • ULM
ARCHETYPE: BRAUN SK4 / T3

Industrial Functionalism

Understated engineering where design is as little design as possible. Stripping away superficial decoration in favor of intuitive ergonomics and honest manufacturing.

Materials: Matte Anodized Aluminum, Bakelite, ABS
Form: Orthogonal Chassis, Dial Ergonomics
SWATCH
1950s – 1970s UK • INTNL
ARCHETYPE: BÉTON BRUT

Architectural & Raw Brutalism

Unapologetic honesty of materials celebrating unfinished surfaces, heavy geometric masses, and visible structural infrastructure without decorative pretense.

Materials: Béton Brut Concrete, Rebar, Timber Cast
Form: Massive Cantilevers, Monolithic Weight
SWATCH
1930s – Contemp. NORDIC • KYOTO
ARCHETYPE: WISHBONE CH24

Scandinavian & Japandi Synthesis

Warm minimalist living honoring organic warmth, craft imperfection (Wabi-sabi), and ergonomic tranquility. Spaces and objects that breathe human comfort.

Materials: White Oak, Paper Cord, Stoneware
Form: Sculpted Curves, Low-slung Profiles
SWATCH
1945 – 1969 CALIFORNIA • MILAN
ARCHETYPE: EAMES LOUNGE 670

Mid-Century Modernism

Post-war optimism blending industrial production with sculptural organic silhouettes. Clean lines, fluid transitions, and seamless indoor-outdoor harmony.

Materials: Molded Rosewood, Fiberglass, Spun Brass
Form: Sculptural Curves, Biomorphic Volumes
SWATCH
2020s – Future SPATIAL • GLOBAL
ARCHETYPE: SPATIAL GLASS

Spatial & Dimensional UI

Multi-layered dynamic interfaces utilizing real-time specular lighting, physical material refraction, micro-depth cards, and tactile fluid interactions.

Materials: Frosted Acrylic, OLED Black, Refraction
Form: 2.5D Layering, Spring Motion Curves
SWATCH
1917 – 1931 LEIDEN • UTRECHT
ARCHETYPE: RED & BLUE CHAIR

De Stijl / Neoplasticism

Pure abstraction and universality achieved by reducing visual vocabulary to perpendicular horizontal and vertical lines, primary hues, and non-colors.

Materials: Lacquered Beechwood, Painted Plywood, Steel
Form: Orthogonal Grids, Intersecting Planes
SWATCH
1920s – 1930s NEW YORK • PARIS
ARCHETYPE: COLDSPOT SUPER SIX

Art Deco / Streamline Moderne

Machine-age elegance celebrating velocity, aerodynamic curves, and industrial glamour. Frictionless teardrop hulls and horizontal speed lines applied to everyday appliances.

Materials: Polished Chrome, Porcelain Enamel, Bakelite
Form: Aerodynamic Curves, Speed Striations
SWATCH
1960s – 1970s TOKYO • GINZA
ARCHETYPE: NAKAGIN CAPSULE

Japanese Metabolism / Wabi Modern

Post-war avant-garde architecture fusing biological regeneration cycles with replaceable prefabricated living capsules anchored to permanent megastructure cores.

Materials: Welded Steel Trusses, FRP Molded Pods, Concrete
Form: Plug-in Cellular Modules, Circular Apertures
SWATCH
1981 – 1987 MILAN • ITALY
ARCHETYPE: CARLTON BOOKCASE

Memphis Design & Radical Postmodernism

Subversive rebellion against austere corporate functionalism, unleashing vibrant plastic laminates, Bacterio graphic patterns, and playful geometric totems.

Materials: Print HPL Laminate, Terrazzo, Lacquered MDF
Form: Asymmetric Totems, Clashing Geometries
